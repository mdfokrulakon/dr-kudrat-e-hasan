The heart-healthy plate

A simple way to fill your plate

  • Half: vegetables and fruit. Shak, lau, patal, brinjal, cauliflower, tomato, cucumber, guava, papaya, orange.
  • A quarter: whole grains. Ata roti, brown rice or oats. Keep portions of white rice moderate.
  • A quarter: protein. Fish, dal, beans, chickpeas, eggs, or skinless chicken.

Food guide

What to eat and what to limit

✓Enjoy these

  • Vegetables and leafy greensAim for a variety of colours every day, including shak.
  • FruitWhole fruit is better than juice. Guava, papaya, orange, apple and seasonal fruit.
  • Fish, at least twice a weekOily fish such as hilsa, sardine or mackerel, grilled, steamed or cooked in little oil.
  • Dal, beans and chickpeasGood plant protein and fibre, cooked with little oil and salt.
  • Whole grainsAta roti, oats, brown rice.
  • A small handful of unsalted nutsAlmonds, walnuts or peanuts, not salted or fried.
  • Healthier cooking oils, in small amountsSuch as soybean, sunflower, rice bran or olive oil.
  • WaterPlain water or unsweetened tea instead of sugary drinks.

✕Limit these

  • Extra saltSalt added at the table, pickles (achar), dried fish (shutki), salty snacks and instant noodles.
  • Deep-fried foodsSingara, samosa, puri, pakora, fried chicken, and oil that has been reused many times.
  • Vanaspati and daldaThese can contain trans fats. Also check bakery biscuits, cakes and pastries.
  • Heavy ghee and fatty meatRich biryani, tehari or kacchi, visible fat and chicken skin. Choose lean meat in small portions.
  • Organ meatsLiver, brain and similar foods are high in cholesterol.
  • Sugary foods and drinksSoft drinks, packaged juice, sweetened tea, mishti, sweets and desserts.
  • Processed meatSausages, salami and nuggets.
  • Large, late, heavy dinnersHave a lighter evening meal where you can.

Diets differ for people with diabetes, kidney disease, or those taking blood thinners such as warfarin. Please ask the doctor before making big changes.

Easy swaps

Small changes that add up

Fried singara or samosa→Roasted chickpeas or a piece of fruit
Extra salt on rice or salad→Lemon, herbs, garlic, green chilli
Soft drink or sweet tea→Water, lemon water, or unsweetened tea
Fried fish→Steamed, grilled or lightly cooked fish
Paratha or puri→Ata roti with vegetable curry
Vanaspati or dalda→A small amount of a vegetable oil

Daily habits

What to do for a healthier heart

150 min / week

Move your body

At least 150 minutes of moderate activity, such as brisk walking. That is 30 minutes on five days.

0 tobacco

Stay tobacco-free

Cigarettes, bidi, hookah and smokeless tobacco such as jorda and gul all harm the heart. Ask for help quitting.

7–9 hours

Sleep well

Regular, good-quality sleep supports healthy blood pressure and weight.

<5 g salt

Cut back on salt

Less than one teaspoon of salt a day in total, including salt in packaged and preserved food.

♥ checks

Check regularly

Have your blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol checked as the doctor advises, even if you feel well.

💊 medicines

Take medicines as prescribed

Never stop or change heart or blood pressure medicines without asking your doctor. Avoid self-medicating.

☺ calm

Manage stress

Make time for rest, family, prayer or hobbies, and talk to someone if you feel overwhelmed.

⚖ weight

Keep a healthy weight

Smaller portions and daily movement help. Avoid alcohol, or keep it low.

If you get chest pain, pressure or sudden breathlessness: stop what you are doing, sit down, and call 999 or ask someone to take you to hospital. Do not drive yourself.Call 999

Example only

A sample heart-friendly day

Breakfast morning

Ata roti or oats with vegetable bhaji, or a boiled egg, plus a piece of fruit.

Lunch midday

A moderate portion of rice, dal, a leafy vegetable, vegetable curry, a piece of fish and a fresh salad.

Snack afternoon

Seasonal fruit, or a small handful of unsalted nuts or roasted chickpeas.

Dinner evening

Lighter: roti with vegetables and dal, fish or skinless chicken. Eat earlier if you can.

An example, not a prescription. Your doctor or a dietitian can tailor a plan to your health.
